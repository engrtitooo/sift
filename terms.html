2. What Sift does — and what it cannot promise

Sift finds the screenshots already in your photo library, reads each one, and gives you back a title, a short summary, and the useful details it found: a date, a place, a price, a link, a code. It then offers one action per screenshot — add to calendar, save the place, track the product, set a reminder, keep a note, or delete the picture.

The reading is done by an AI model, and it is not always right

Every summary and every extracted field comes from a machine reading an image. It can misread a digit, pick the wrong date out of a busy screenshot, summarise something in a way you would not have, or miss a detail entirely. Sift is a way to find things again, not a system of record. Before you act on anything that matters — catching a flight, paying a price, entering a code, meeting someone at a time — check it against the screenshot itself, which Sift leaves exactly where it was.

Actions do what they say and nothing more

When you tap an action, Sift writes one calendar event, or schedules one local notification, or copies one piece of text. It never reads your existing calendar events, and it never changes or deletes anything already in your calendar. Reminders are local to your phone — we have no server that can send you a notification, and nothing leaves your phone to make one fire.

Deleting a photo

Sift can delete a screenshot from your photo library, and this is the one action that cannot be undone from inside the app. Sift asks you to confirm every single time, and iOS shows its own confirmation on top of ours. A deleted photo goes to your Recently Deleted album under the rules iOS sets, not ours. We are not able to recover a photo for you.

3. What you need to use it

Sift needs an iPhone running a supported version of iOS, permission to read your photo library, and an internet connection at the moment a screenshot is read. Calendar and notification actions need their own permissions, which the app asks for only when you first use the action that needs them, and which you can refuse without losing the rest of the app.

Without a network connection Sift can still show you everything it has already read, because that lives on your phone. It cannot read anything new.

6. Deep Scan — the one-time pack

A Deep Scan is a one-time purchase, not a subscription. It does not renew, it is not billed again, and cancelling nothing is required because there is nothing running.

It grants enough reads to go through the screenshots you already have, in one pass — sized to your actual library, with a minimum of 3,000 and a maximum of 10,000 reads per pack. If your library holds more than 10,000 screenshots, a second pack adds another 10,000 on top. If the pass is interrupted, it picks up where it left off. Unused reads stay yours; they do not expire with the month.

What it is not: a Deep Scan does not cover screenshots you take after you buy it. It clears the backlog; Sift Pro keeps up with what arrives next. They are complements, not alternatives, and we would rather say so here than have you find out a month later.

To size your pack, the app tells our server one number — how many screenshots it found on your phone — once, when the scan first goes through your library after you buy. Nothing else about your library is sent: no filenames, no dates, no content. If you never buy a Deep Scan, that number is never sent. The Privacy Policy covers this in full.

The US list price at the time of writing is $9.99. As with the subscriptions, the App Store price for your country is the one you pay.
